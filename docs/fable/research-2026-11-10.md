# Research for the portfolio redesign (2026-11-10)

Gathered by two web scouts for Fable 5.2, the owner of this portfolio. "(snippet)" means the point was seen only in a search result, not on the opened page. Gaps are listed at the end of each part; fill them yourself if a decision depends on them.

## Owner's taste (from his notes)

He plays Marvel Rivals and Genshin Impact, and trains aim in Kovaak's. He also built Whisker Gacha, a cat gacha game. He lives with 13 cats. Stylized, colourful, character-driven art suits him better than realism.

## Owner's direction for the theme (2026-11-10)

- Art style: Marvel Rivals-like comic shading ("2D mindset": bold outlines, flat colour, halftone and ink accents). Original characters and art only.
- World: small planet-islands floating in space, one per project or group, inspired by the planetoid idea in Mario Galaxy. Borrow the concept only, never its characters, art, music or look.
- Movement: 2D (2.5D). The models are 3D, but the player moves along a fixed path or plane (around a planet, hop to the next). No free exploration. Guide the visitor instead of letting them get lost; it should never be annoying.
- Accessibility (WCAG) matters more than "explorability".
- Why: this sets it clearly apart from Bruno Simon's free-roaming driving island.

Game-feel starting values (practitioner blogs, rough): vibration 0.08–0.12 s, flashes 50–100 ms, shake about 300 ms. Classic references: the GDC talks "Juice it or Lose it" and "The Art of Screenshake".

Cross-cutting principles:
1. One distinctive palette and one strong reference, kept everywhere.
2. Stylize with flat or toon shading and outlines, not realism; it ages better and runs on phones.
3. Each interaction gives 2–3 feedback channels (motion, sound, number), scaled to the event.
4. Colour is the label: one colour per project category.
5. One persistent visual signature.
6. Silhouettes and landmarks must read from far away.
7. Simple geometry, low-res or toon tricks, instancing.

## Part 2: popular 3D portfolios and how they handle UX

- **Bruno Simon, 2026 rebuild** (Awwwards case study, read): still an island you drive around, aiming for "ten times better" than 2019. Mobile switches automatically to a lower-quality preset. Models compressed with DRACO, textures with ETC1S/UASTC (KTX2). Heavy instancing for trees, foliage and benches; frustum culling; water blur and depth of field off on mobile. Portfolio Honors Dec 2025, Site of the Month Jan 2026 (snippet). https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html
  **Note:** this repo is already a Bruno-style driving island. A redesign has to look and play differently from his, not like a smaller copy.
- **Codrops, June 2026:** "a 3D portfolio built without a single 3D model" (HTTP 403, snippet only). Relevant because this repo is fully procedural. https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/06/11/sketching-the-impossible-a-3d-portfolio-built-without-a-single-3d-model/

Recruiter and developer complaints (forum and Reddit, snippets): load time is the top complaint; recruiters "are usually not coders" and want the information fast; offer a "download resume as PDF" button so busy reviewers can skip the game; unclear touch controls on mobile.

Rules for a junior developer's 3D portfolio aimed at getting hired:
1. A plain project list, resume (PDF) and contact on the first screen or one click away. The 3D world is never the only path.
2. DRACO for models, KTX2 for textures, instancing and culling.
3. A real progress loading screen and a small asset budget, so the first interactive view comes fast.
4. Label the controls; show the input hint on touch devices.
5. Detect weak devices and drop to a low preset (already in this repo).
6. Keep physics only where gameplay needs it.
7. Each project shows what was built, the stack and the result in a few seconds, with Live and Code links.
